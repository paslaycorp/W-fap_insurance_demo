Public Research Overview

Frontier Research Department (FRD)

Independent research and systems development for defensible decisions under changing evidence, authority, and time.

MISSION

FRD develops and evaluates systems that determine whether available evidence legitimately supports a decision or state change, preserve what was known and justified at the time, and prevent later information from silently rewriting earlier decision context.

Research domains

  • Evidentiary provenance and integrity
  • Decision assurance and authority boundaries
  • Temporal integrity and historical reconstruction
  • Uncertainty, contradiction, and revocation handling
  • Adversarial and counterfactual evaluation
  • Trustworthy automated and agentic decision systems

Systems in development

  • EPM — provenance, state, and temporal integrity
  • TVC — temporal and counterfactual research
  • Governor — admissibility and authority boundaries
  • Variant Hunter — adversarial testing and edge discovery
  • FAP-Core — applied decision-assurance workflows

Public-safe principles

  • Claims should not exceed the evidence, provenance, authority, and boundaries supporting them.
  • Later knowledge must not be laundered into earlier decision context.
  • Missing evidence, explicit revocation, and invalid authority are distinct states and should remain distinguishable.
  • Auditability requires preserving both the decision and the conditions that made it justified or unjustified at that time.

Current validation and benchmark work

Public synthetic benchmarks exercise valid approval, required deferral, explicit revocation after approval, and reconstruction of earlier justification. Evaluation considers both unsafe approvals and unnecessary blocking. Where independent verification has not occurred, FRD does not claim it.

Public Synthetic Validation Artifact

SYN-AUTH-01 — Decision integrity across time

Illustrative benchmark contract. Expected outcomes are specified in advance. This page does not claim independent validation or production execution.

Frozen public rule

PAY-AUTH-v1.0: a payment above $1,000 requires a verified approval record bound to the invoice, amount, and an authorized Finance approver. An explicit valid revocation removes present authorization but does not rewrite whether earlier authorization was valid when it existed.

Fixture: SYN-AUTH-01 · Synthetic $1,250 invoice

Output: decision | execution_permitted | reason_code | evidence_refs | rule_ref

T0 · 2026-10-25 22:30 UTC
DEFER

Invoice and purchase match are present. No qualifying approval record is available. Execution is not permitted because required authorization evidence is missing.

Evidence state: Invoice + purchase match; approval absent.

T1 · 2026-10-25 22:35 UTC
ALLOW

A verified current approval from an authorized Finance approver is available and bound to the invoice and amount. Present execution is permitted under the rule.

Evidence state: T0 evidence + qualifying approval.

T2 · 2026-10-25 22:40 UTC
DENY

A valid explicit revocation is now present. Current authorization is withdrawn. The T1 record remains historically valid as the earlier decision context.

Evidence state: T1 evidence + explicit revocation.

What this benchmark is intended to test

  • Correctly distinguish missing evidence (DEFER) from explicit loss of authorization (DENY).
  • Permit action when the frozen rule is satisfied, rather than proving safety by refusing everything.
  • Preserve T1 justification after T2 revocation instead of retroactively rewriting history.
  • Return the rule and evidence references supporting each decision in a reproducible output contract.

Epistemic status: Expected behavior is part of the benchmark contract. Independent comparative results, measured performance, and production guarantees are not claimed on this artifact.
